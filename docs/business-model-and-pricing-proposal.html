Commercial strategy

Business model and pricing proposal

An assessment of consumer software, manufacturer commerce, and a professional pay-per-project model for interactive client walkthroughs and VR.

Proposal 10 September 2026 Infrastructure proposal

Executive view

The professional client-delivery proposition is the stronger starting business. The £50-per-house idea is clear and testable, but should be framed as a publishing licence rather than the entire long-term price of the software.

✓

Recommended starting point

Sell architects and builders an authenticated workspace that turns a house project into a branded, immutable, read-only client link with walkthrough and VR. Add manufacturer products and consumer access after the professional workflow has proven demand.

Market context

Consumers can already access capable home-design products cheaply or free. Planner 5D offers a free tier, consumer Premium at $59.99 annually, and Professional at $399.99 annually. RoomSketcher lists Pro at $144 annually and Team at $420 annually. SketchUp Pro is approximately £29 per person per month when billed annually.

Implication: House Designer should not compete as simply another paid drawing tool. Its differentiation should be the connected workflow: accurate house modelling, curated real products, an effortless client link, and useful VR.

Option 1 — consumer software and commerce

The consumer model can become a valuable marketplace, but it asks the company to establish users, manufacturers, catalogue quality, attribution, and AI quality simultaneously.

More active users
More manufacturer interest
Better product catalogue
More designs and purchases

The challenge is starting the loop. Before meaningful consumer traffic exists, manufacturers have limited incentive to prepare models, maintain product data, integrate tracking, or negotiate commission.

CPC

Paid click model

A manufacturer pays a fixed fee for each qualified outbound click. This requires reliable click measurement, fraud controls, reporting, and a sufficiently valuable audience.

CPA / affiliate

Sales commission model

The platform receives a percentage of an attributed, completed sale. It requires stable product identifiers, deep links, attribution windows, consent, and adjustment for cancellations or returns.

“Shop this look” is a strong product feature, but manufacturer commission should initially be treated as future upside—not the source expected to fund the first version of the platform.

Option 2 — professional publishing

The clearest proposition is: “Turn this project into a branded, interactive client presentation with an exclusive browser link and VR walkthrough.”

  1. The practice builds and reviews the house in its authenticated workspace.
  2. A designer deliberately publishes an immutable client revision.
  3. The client receives a branded, unguessable URL.
  4. The link opens immediately on desktop or mobile with simple controls.
  5. On a compatible headset, the same HTTPS link provides Enter VR.

A mandatory app download would add installation, compatibility, and version-management friction. The browser link should be the primary experience; optional PWA installation or an offline archive can be introduced later.

What a £50 publishing licence should include

  • One read-only client URL
  • Walkthrough and VR modes
  • Practice branding
  • A defined number of published revisions
  • Passcode or named-client access
  • Basic open and VR-start analytics
  • A stated hosting term, such as 12 or 24 months
  • Archive/export or paid renewal when hosting expires
Important: charge for a published project, not every internal revision. Continuing design work should not alter the signed-off client version until the practice explicitly republishes it.

Is £50 enough?

It is an excellent pilot price and an easy cost for a practice to pass through to its client. It may ultimately be too low if the product becomes part of the practice’s normal workflow. A firm publishing 30 houses annually would generate only £1,500 in revenue, even if the system saves that firm many hours or helps win additional instructions.

CDN delivery should keep infrastructure cost per view relatively low. Customer onboarding, professional support, browser and headset compatibility, sales, and long-lived project retention will matter more than raw bandwidth.

How manufacturers fit

The manufacturer catalogue remains strategically valuable in the professional model, but it becomes an enhancement rather than a launch dependency.

Early catalogue

  • Ship a useful curated generic library.
  • Allow firms to upload private bespoke assets.
  • Invite a small group of manufacturers already specified by pilot firms.
  • Track product placement in real projects.

Later manufacturer revenue

  • Paid enhanced listings or product families
  • Specification and engagement analytics
  • Qualified leads
  • Clearly labelled sponsored placement
  • Affiliate commission on consumer purchases

Manufacturers should initially receive basic listings without charge. Charging becomes credible once House Designer can demonstrate an engaged professional or consumer audience and measurable product interaction.

Recommended path to market

  1. Step 1Sell the professional client-publishing workflow.
  2. Step 2Test £50 per published house with five to ten local architecture or building firms.
  3. Step 3Measure whether firms prefer pay-as-you-go, monthly billing, or a hybrid.
  4. Step 4Add branded links, immutable revisions, access controls, and viewer analytics.
  5. Step 5Add firm-private asset uploads and a reliable production conversion pipeline.
  6. Step 6Recruit manufacturers based on products the pilot firms already specify.
  7. Step 7Open a consumer project pass after the catalogue and viewer are mature.
  8. Step 8Add “Shop this look” and AI furnishing once engagement proves demand.

Positioning

House Designer helps architects and builders turn plans into interactive client walkthroughs and VR presentations. Firms design and publish; clients explore; manufacturers supply real products that can eventually be specified or purchased.

This is more differentiated than another inexpensive consumer floor-planning application. It gives each participant a clear reason to use the platform and allows the professional product to grow naturally into the public marketplace.
