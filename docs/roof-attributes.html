01 / User guide

Getting started

Joining and matching solve different problems. A join resolves the geometry where roofs intersect. A height match keeps a chosen ridge, eave or slope aligned. Many roof arrangements need both.

This guide explains the current roof controls, what changes in the model when you use them, and how to build common roof arrangements. Start with the shape and supporting points, then set the height and joins. Use automatic matching when roofs should remain aligned as you edit them.

Controls and examples checked against the working application on 6 October 2026. The diagrams are schematic; they explain the settings rather than reproduce the house geometry.

The words used in the controls

Ridge is the highest line where roof faces meet. Eave is the lower edge of a roof slope. Gable is the end face beneath two slopes. A chamfer replaces part of a gable tip with a sloping roof face.

Overhang is the horizontal extension beyond the supporting walls. Fascia is the exposed edge board; soffit is the surface beneath an overhang. The support footprint is the area established by the mounting points.

02 / User guide

Choosing and placing a roof

A new roof starts in Shape. The shape selector is available during creation. Selecting an existing roof opens its settings for editing; the shared edit panel does not offer a shape selector.

ShapeWhat it createsUseful for
GableTwo slopes meeting along a ridge, with gable ends.Main roofs, projecting gables and roofs with unequal eave heights.
HippedRoof faces slope down towards all four sides.A roof without exposed vertical gable ends.
Lean-toOne sloping roof face. When attached to a wall, that mounting wall is the high edge.Extensions, porches and roofs against a taller facade.
FlatA horizontal roof surface. There is no pitch control.Flat roof sections.
Bay roofFacets over a convex bay outline, with an optional ridge projecting from the rear mounting edge.Angled or rectangular bay projections.

Mounting points and direction

  1. Work on the floor that owns the roof. Choose its shape, pitch and overhangs.
  2. Select the supporting points on the plan. For rectangular roof types these establish an oriented rectangular support area. The roof does not trace every corner of an irregular footprint.
  3. Use Rotate 90 where shown, or the hipped roof Direction selector, to orient the preview. Inspect the ridge or high edge before creating the roof.
  4. For a bay, select the two rear points against the house first, followed by the outer corners. All outer points must lie on the same side of that rear edge. The outline must form a valid convex bay.
  5. Check the preview, then choose Create. Clear removes the current placement points so you can start again.

Blue horizontal and vertical guides help align a new mounting point with points already selected. Hold Ctrl for free placement without those alignment snaps. These guides help plan alignment; they do not link roof heights.

Plan labels: End A and End B identify the two ridge ends. Side 1 and Side 2 identify the two sides of a gable. Their identities rotate with the roof, so use the selected plan labels rather than assuming a side is always north or south.

03 / User guide

Pitch and overhangs

Pitch

Pitch (°) is the angle of a roof slope above horizontal. The control accepts 1° to 75° for pitched roofs. Increasing it normally raises the ridge for a fixed support width. Flat roofs ignore this attribute.

For a regular centred gable, each slope has half the support width as its horizontal run. The rise is horizontal run × tan(pitch). A 6 m wide gable at 33° therefore rises about 1.95 m above its support datum. This formula is a useful check, not a value you need to calculate to place a roof.

The entered pitch is not always the effective pitch on every face. A mounted side of an unequal-eave gable can adjust its pitch to meet a fixed ridge. Bay facet pitches depend on the outline; the selected pitch controls the rise from the limiting support edge. Check the effective side readouts for an asymmetric gable.

Overhang distances

Ends (m) extends the roof beyond its two end boundaries. On a gable, these are the gable ends. Side 1 (m) and Side 2 (m) independently extend its eaves. Other shapes use a shared side/eaves distance. A bay overhang follows its exposed edges and leaves the rear mounting edge against the house.

Distances are measured in plan, horizontally, not along the sloping tiles. For example, 0.30 means a 300 mm horizontal overhang. Increasing an overhang adds roof outside the supports; it does not widen the supporting walls.

Overhang pitch

Leave Use roof pitch for overhangs checked for a continuous slope. On an unequal-eave gable it normally follows each side’s effective pitch. The linked mounted-roof case described under Construction and finishes can inherit the target overhang pitch to keep equal-width eaves aligned.

Uncheck it to enter a separate Overhang pitch (°), from 0° to 75°. This changes only the part beyond the support edge. A different angle creates a deliberate bend at the wall line. Equal overhang widths do not guarantee equal outer eave heights if the starting heights or slopes differ.

04 / User guide

Gables with unequal eave heights

Enable Unequal eave heights for a gable that has a shared ridge but different eave levels. This exposes ridge height and eave readouts in Height & joins, and the ridge offset and constraint in Advanced. Newly enabled roofs default to keeping both side pitches; older saved roofs can retain a mounted-side constraint.

The pitch and eave constraint

Choice in AdvancedWhat stays fixedWhat can change
Keep both side pitches
(free eaves)		Both main slopes use the entered pitch; the ridge height is retained.Eave heights vary with ridge offset and support width.
Side 1 or Side 2That side meets the normal wall-top datum, including vertical offset.Its pitch adjusts to reach the ridge. The opposite side keeps the entered pitch.
Shorter side
(automatic)		The side with the shorter ridge-to-support run is mounted at the datum.Its effective pitch can differ from the entered pitch.

Ridge offset

Ridge offset (m) is sideways displacement from the support centre. Positive moves towards Side 2; negative moves towards Side 1. The ridge stays at its chosen height. The allowed offset keeps it at least 50 mm inside either support edge.

With free eaves and a fixed ridge, moving towards Side 1 shortens that slope and raises its eave; it lengthens Side 2 and lowers that eave. Automatic eave matching can calculate the offset for you.

Read the results before changing more settings

Side pitches shows the actual main-slope angles. Eaves above floor shows heights at the support edges, before the outer overhang fall. These are readouts, not editable inputs. If you want both sides at 33°, select Keep both side pitches; mounting one side can legitimately produce a different angle.

05 / User guide

Setting and matching roof heights

Roof height normally starts from the owning floor’s elevation + room height. This is the normal wall-top datum, not a separate height stored in every selected mounting point. A roof’s vertical offset then shifts it from that datum.

Ridge height on an unequal eave gable

Choose Manual height and enter Above wall tops (m), or choose Match [floor] · Roof [number]. A height link follows the target roof’s highest original profile point in world coordinates, including floor elevation and offset. On an ordinary gable that point is its ridge. Targets can be on another floor.

The manual field displays the ridge height relative to the normal wall tops, including any vertical offset. With a manual ridge, a later vertical-offset change moves the whole roof. A linked ridge stays anchored to the target’s world height. Unlinking keeps the matched result.

Align with another roof

This control is available after creating and selecting a gable. Choose the alignment type, then its Match roof target. A joined-end target or ridge-height target may be preselected, but check that it is the intended roof.

AlignmentThe app adjustsRequirements and meaning
Match adjoining slopeVertical offset of the whole selected roof.An adjoining face with the same pitch and direction. The original face bounds must overlap or be within 0.5 m in plan.
Match side 1 eaveRidge offset.Matches the selected outer eave to the nearest suitable target eave. Enables unequal eaves and keeps both pitches.
Match side 2 eaveRidge offset.Same rule for Side 2. Ridge height and entered pitches remain fixed.
Manual settingsRemoves the alignment link.Keeps the current calculated height and ridge settings for further manual editing.

The calculated offset is locked while its alignment is resolved. Eave matching includes the overhang fall, so it matches the outer tile edge, rather than the support-edge readout. It does not require you to type a tiny corrective decimal.

Match height and join geometry separately. A ridge or eave link aligns levels; use a gable connection to establish the roof intersection. Matching cannot make incompatible slopes continuous, and a gable with a linked ridge cannot also be translated by slope matching. Use eave alignment for that case.

Missing targets, circular links or impossible ridge positions report a warning and use manual fallback settings. Break a circular link by making one roof the independent reference. Successful links are saved and follow later edits.

06 / User guide

Chamfering a gable end

Open Chamfered ends in Shape and enable Chamfer end A or Chamfer end B. This replaces part of the vertical gable tip with a sloping roof face. Each end has its own settings.

Angle and setback work together

Angle (°) is the chamfer face’s angle above horizontal, from 1° to 75°. Setback (m) is the horizontal distance along the roof length from the outer end edge to where the chamfer meets the ridge. It is not a vertical drop or distance measured up the slope.

At a fixed angle, increasing the setback moves the ridge intersection further inwards and lowers the chamfer at the end edge. The drop is setback × tan(angle). For example, a 3 m setback at 33° gives about 1.95 m of drop from the ridge to that edge. The main side pitches are retained.

Match higher side eave

Enable Match higher side eave when the chamfer should end at the higher outer side eave level. The app calculates setback from the ridge height, chamfer angle and eave level. The manual setback is hidden in the Shape panel while this is active.

This follows later geometry changes and is the simplest option for a chamfer that should meet an adjoining gable slope. Turn it off if the chamfer should go further and lower the wall below that eave level. Turning it off retains the effective setback as a starting value.

Limits and wall fitting

The limit is the remaining roof length, including its end extents. There is no fixed 2.6 m limit and no requirement to stop at the original wall-top height. If both ends are chamfered, they can meet but cannot overlap; each reduces the other’s available length.

The control shows the effective limit. Oversized values in a saved project are limited during construction; the renderer does not rewrite the authored file. Enable Fit supporting walls to roof when a deep chamfer drops below the wall tops, so the walls and their caps follow the underside.

A chamfer controls the shape of one roof. It does not choose a receiving roof or match a ridge by itself. Configure those relationships in Height & joins.

07 / User guide

Joining roofs together

Open Gable connections in Height & joins. A gable’s End A and End B are independent. Their labels follow the roof when it rotates. These controls appear after the roof has been created.

End choiceBehaviour
AutomaticLooks for a single nearby gable with a crossing ridge direction. The support end must overlap or be within 350 mm. Ambiguous targets need an explicit choice.
Exposed gableKeeps the authored end and its overhang without extending towards a target. Existing portions inside another roof can still be hidden.
Join roof…Stores an explicit target. Can reach a more distant roof, including one on another floor. Choose the receiving roof in Connect to.

What a join changes

A join resolves the overlapping slopes and the boundary between the roofs. It can extend the incoming gable towards the receiving ridge without changing its saved footprint or pitch. The extension is bounded by the receiving support footprint so it does not continue through the far slope or escape past the receiving gable.

Joined pairs trim their overlapping slopes even when the receiving roof has Clip house geometry turned off. This is permission for that relationship; it does not allow the pair to cut unrelated walls or slabs. A receiving roof can therefore protect its gable face while participating in the intersection along its side slope.

Height still matters

At an automatic junction the higher ridge normally continues through; a lower or equal incoming roof extends towards it. An explicit join can also extend a higher incoming ridge. Ridge heights within 25 mm are treated as equal for connection priority. This tolerance is not an instruction to flatten genuine height differences.

A height match does not create a join, and a join does not force equal pitches or eave levels. For a smooth continuation, set compatible pitches, add the join, then use Match adjoining slope or Match side eave as appropriate.

Tiles and fascia at the intersection

The 3D view constructs closed roof pieces and removes internal faces at the resolved intersection. It derives exposed edges, soffits and fascia from the surviving roof surfaces. Touching joined surfaces within 1 mm of the same plane can share a finish plane to remove tiny seams. Larger steps remain part of the geometry.

If a target is moved away, deleted or unsuitable, the saved explicit connection remains and shows a warning. Choose another target or change that end to Exposed gable. Adding a join is not a substitute for correcting a misplaced support footprint.

08 / User guide

Construction details and wall behaviour

Vertical offset

Vertical offset (m) is in Advanced. Zero uses the normal wall-top datum; negative lowers the roof and positive raises it. For example, -0.20 lowers it by 200 mm. The footprint and entered pitch stay unchanged. A resolved slope alignment makes this field read-only; a linked ridge has the special behaviour described under Height matching.

Wall fitting and house clipping are separate

ControlWhat it doesWhen to use it
Fit supporting walls to roofTrims and caps eligible supporting external wall tops and that floor’s horizontal room ceiling beneath the roof. Preserves saved wall heights and openings.A lowered eave or deep chamfer sits below the wall tops.
Clip house geometryAllows the roof to trim intersecting house geometry, including walls, ceilings/slabs and other roofs under the resolver’s rules. On by default.A roof defines the building envelope. Turn off for a roof that should fit against the house while leaving its geometry independent.

Wall fitting works even with general clipping off. It leaves taller adjoining facades, walls continuing outside the supported region, and other floors independent. Turning fitting off restores the authored wall shape. Fitting trims existing walls; raising a roof alone does not extend those authored wall heights. Roof-owned gable or supporting infill may close eligible spans beneath a gable.

Thickness and finishes

Roof thickness (m) sets the constructed roof skin thickness, from 0.01 to 1 m. An unspecified roof uses 0.04 m. Thickness changes the underside and edge closure; it is not the depth of a complete real-world roof build-up.

Soffit / fascia colour sets the default edge and underside finish where this control is shown, currently on gable and bay roofs. The default is white. Roof surface materials, texture scale and orientation are assigned through the Materials workflow and stored separately from the roof’s geometry attributes.

A special case for linked overhangs

A mounted unequal-eave gable sharing a ridge and wall-top datum with a symmetric gable can automatically use that target’s overhang pitch. Equal-width eaves can then meet even if the mounted main slope is steeper. An explicit overhang pitch or a free-eave constraint keeps its own settings. Check those settings before diagnosing a bend as a rendering defect.

09 / User guide

Examples for starting a design

A regular gable

  1. Choose Gable (two slopes). Set pitch to 33°, end overhang to 0.20 m and both side overhangs to 0.30 m.
  2. Leave Unequal eave heights off. Leave Use roof pitch for overhangs on. Select the four support corners of a 6 m wide rectangular area and create the roof.
  3. The centred ridge is about 1.95 m above the support datum. The outer eaves are about 0.195 m lower than the support edges because of the 300 mm overhang.
  4. Use Exposed gable on a free-standing end if an automatic nearby connection is unwanted. Paint the roof using Materials.

Lowering a porch roof onto its walls

  1. Create a lean-to against its mounting wall and check that the wall is the high edge in the preview.
  2. In Advanced, set Vertical offset to -0.20 m. This lowers the roof by 200 mm without changing its entered pitch.
  3. In Height & joins, enable Fit supporting walls to roof. Inspect the wall caps and ceiling in 3D.
  4. If this porch should be subordinate to the main house, turn Clip house geometry off. Wall fitting can remain on. This does not turn off an explicit joined pair’s own intersection rules.

A bay roof with a ridge

  1. Choose Bay roof. Select the two points against the house, followed by the outer bay corners, so the preview points away from the mounting edge.
  2. Set pitch to 33°, eaves overhang to 0.20 m and Ridge length to 0.50 m. Provided the bay is deep enough, the ridge runs 500 mm from the rear midpoint towards the bay front.
  3. Set Ridge length to 0 for a pointed arrangement. Increase it for a longer ridge. The maximum depends on the convex outline and retains a 50 mm margin before the support boundary.
  4. Check the result from above and from the front. Changing ridge length can also change the computed roof height because the remaining run to the support edges changes.

These are demonstration values, not project defaults. Place and inspect each roof before adding more. Establish a main reference roof before linking secondary roofs to it.

10 / User guide

The roof arrangement in your house

The supplied colin_house_v2_aligned_roofs.json expresses the successful measured arrangement as relationships. On Floor 1, Roof 1 is the rear gable above the bifolds, Roof 2 is the unequal-eave cross roof, and Roof 3 is the smaller front gable. These numbers describe that saved file; roof numbers in another design can differ.

RoofShape settingsHeight relationship
1 Rear gable29° pitch; continuous overhang slope.Independent reference roof.
2 Cross roof33° pitch on both sides; unequal eaves; Keep both side pitches. Chamfer end B at 33° with Match higher side eave.Ridge matches Roof 1. Side 1 outer eave matches Roof 1.
3 Front gable33° pitch; continuous overhang slope.Match adjoining slope to Roof 2, continuing its chamfer face.

Recreating the relationships through the controls

  1. Set Roof 1 to 29°. Keep it as the independent reference rather than linking it back to Roof 2.
  2. On Roof 2, set pitch to 33° and enable Unequal eave heights. In Advanced choose Keep both side pitches (free eaves).
  3. In Height & joins, set Ridge height to match Floor 1 · Roof 1. Then set Align with another roof to Match side 1 eave and choose Roof 1.
  4. On Roof 2, enable Chamfer end B, set its angle to 33° and enable Match higher side eave. Enable Fit supporting walls to roof. In the saved arrangement, Clip house geometry is off for this roof.
  5. On Roof 1, set gable End B to Join roof and choose Roof 2. On Roof 3, also join End B to Roof 2.
  6. Set Roof 3 to 33°. Choose Match adjoining slope with Roof 2 as the target. The app calculates the small vertical correction needed to continue the chamfer.

Keep the measured support footprints and rotations while applying this recipe. Different widths, positions or overhangs produce different solved offsets. Automatic matching cannot reproduce the house from pitch values alone.

What the automatic settings replace

For the saved arrangement, the solved Roof 2 ridge offset is about -0.429 m, its chamfer setback about 3.018 m, and Roof 3’s vertical correction about +19 mm. These are results to inspect, not decimals the user needs to remember or type. The links recalculate them when the reference roofs change.

11 / User guide

Troubleshooting the roof controls

What you seeCheck and action
One side reads 36.5° when Pitch is 33°.Check Advanced → Pitch / eave constraint. A mounted side adjusts to the fixed ridge. Choose Keep both side pitches if both slopes must be 33°.
The eave is too high, but the ridge must stay fixed.Enable unequal eaves and keep both pitches. Link the ridge to the reference, then use Match side 1 or side 2 eave. Confirm the selected side label.
Two roofs meet but leave a height step.A join does not match heights. Check both pitches, support datums and overhang widths. Match adjoining slope for parallel faces, or match an outer eave for a fixed shared ridge.
A chamfer stops too early.Disable Match higher side eave to enter a deeper setback. Check the remaining-length maximum and the opposite end’s chamfer. Enable wall fitting if the deeper face cuts below wall tops.
Walls or ceiling project through a lowered roof.Check Fit supporting walls to roof, the owning floor and support footprint. Taller adjoining facades remain independent. General clipping is a separate control.
Tiles bend at the wall line.Inspect Use roof pitch for overhangs and any explicit overhang angle. On a mounted asymmetric roof, also check effective side pitches and the linked-overhang rule.
An alignment or ridge match reports a warning.Check that the target still exists and there is no circular dependency. Slope matching needs equal pitch and direction; eave matching must keep the ridge inside the support footprint.
A number is disabled or has become a readout.A resolved link is calculating it. Change the relationship or choose Manual settings to retain the result and resume editing.
Fascia appears cut off or there is an isolated fragment.Check footprint, joins and actual height relationships first. If those are correct, save the project and report the geometry defect with the affected roofs and view; do not compensate with arbitrary offsets.

A useful order for adjustments

Check the floor and supports first, then shape and direction, then pitch. Establish the reference ridge, match the intended eaves or slopes, and finally configure chamfers and wall fitting. Inspect overhangs and finishes after those relationships are correct. Changing many independent offsets at once makes it harder to identify the cause.

Saved attributes for shape and placement

This reference maps the controls to the current RoofStructure fields. It is useful when comparing saved projects. Most users should set these through the app: resolved geometry can differ from authored fallback numbers when an automatic link is active.

Saved attributes for heights and relationships

Related settings outside the roof object

The owning floor supplies elevation and roomHeight, which establish the normal roof datum. Individual wall heights, ceiling mode and slab depth affect the building surfaces available for fitting and clipping. Roof material assignments are stored separately and can target the roof surface, underside or gable finishes.
